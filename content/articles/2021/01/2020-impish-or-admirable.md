*Fans of The Office might be gearing up to watch the holiday episode where a fur-clad and dingy Dwight dresses up as Belsnickel and decides if his coworkers have been impish or admirable. We at the Tribune have taken it upon ourselves to pass such judgement on the news of 2020.*

**Politics**

IMPISH: 2020 *Presidential Election: Donald Trump Refuses to Admit Defeat and Ends Up Losing Recount* President Trump and members of the GOP filed 56 lawsuits across the country in attempts to contest the election results. The majority of these cases have been dismissed or withdrawn, and as of Dec. 15, 2020 no court has found an instance of election fraud.

ADMIRABLE: *Kamala Harris Becomes First Vice President Who is a Woman and Person of Color*

Former presidential candidate Harris was a senator from California who served as Attorney General of California prior to her senatorial win. Vice-President Elect Harris graduated from Howard University in 1986 and got her law degree from UC Hastings in 1989.

ADMIRABLE: *14 Year Old Girl Discover Possible Treatment for COVID-19 and Wins \$25K*

A fourteen-year-old girl named Anika Chebrolu from Texas won the 2020 3M Young Scientist Challenge by discovering a lead molecule that can bind to the spike protein of the SARS-CoV-2 virus, which can be used to find a possible therapy for the coronavirus.

**Health**

IMPISH: *Coronavirus Pandemic Wreaks Havoc* As of 4:45 p.m. on Dec. 15, 2020 there have been 73,697, 710 coronavirus cases and 1,638,828 deaths worldwide. The United States leads the world with the most toal cases followed by India and Brazil. In addition to loss of life and serious illness, the coronavirus pandemic has also caused economic devastation and many changes to how we interact with others, attend school and work.*  *

ADMIRABLE: *Revolutionary CRISPR Gene-Editing Technique Shows Promise For Curing Many Diseases* The groundbreaking procedure ushers in an era of gene-editing to treat cancers and rare blood disorders. Victoria Gray, a person living with the blood disorder sickle-cell anemia, was the first person with a genetic disorder to get treated in the United States with the CRISPR technique and the billions of genetically-modified cells she was infused with have been working to alleviate almost all of the sickle-cell complications.
